World Bank · Latin America education · October 2026

AI in education in Latin America: what we are doing and what we have learned.

What each project is, what we have found so far, and what comes next, with the papers, initiative pages and videos behind each one. The work covers four groups (school leaders, teachers, students and whole systems) and an evidence network, EvidencIA, that ties it together. All of it is done jointly with Carolina Lopez in DECRG: it is the knowledge agenda behind our operations.

What we know so far

AI can help or harm learning. The design decides which.

In Turkey, high school students given free access to GPT-4 scored 17 percent lower on an exam taken without it; a version with guardrails removed the harm but produced no gain. When guidance is built in, results turn positive: university students in Chile told how to use AI as a tutor gained 0.22 SD on the final exam, and lower secondary students in Sierra Leone gained 0.26 SD in math with lessons on Gemini's guided mode.

On average, our background paper for the WDR 2026 finds that adaptive and AI-enabled tools raise learning by 0.125 SD across 14 randomized trials, and that generative AI cannot yet be told apart from the adaptive software that came before it.

01 · School leaders

El Director LIBRE

In the latest survey of school leaders, 75 percent of the 1,615 respondents in Latin America (Argentina, Brazil, Chile and Colombia) say they spend most of their time on administrative and management tasks. El Director LIBRE is a free World Bank book with a five-step method for using AI to cut that administrative time so principals can spend it on instructional leadership. Peru's Ministry of Education used it to train about 6,000 new principals.

We have now built it as software, with tools for the paperwork principals actually do (school council minutes, circulars, letters to families), and a version for El Salvador that records how principals use their time. We are now testing the platform with principals so that we can then evaluate it with a randomized trial.

02 · Teachers

Using AI well, and feedback after every lesson

La IA en el Aula

We tested whether training teachers to use AI saves them time. In a randomized trial with 1,269 sixth-grade teachers in 390 public schools in Lima, one day of training, an AI license and weekly messages raised use of the license by 20 percentage points. It saved no detectable time on any of seven main tasks, but teachers wrote better prompts and rejected more weak AI answers, and older teachers gained the most. The lesson for governments: do not buy AI for teachers on the promise of time savings.

Teach + IA

Teach, the Bank's classroom observation tool, needs a certified observer in the room, so most teachers are rarely observed. Using 199 lessons recorded in Peru, we asked whether a language model can pass the same certification exam. With the detailed rubric used to train human observers, it passed 93 percent of simulated exams; with a minimal rubric, only 57 percent. The model was the same: the expertise is in the rubric. Next step: a tool that records a lesson and returns feedback to the teacher, tested with a randomized trial on teaching practice and student learning.

03 · Students

Eligiendo Mi Camino

In Peru's public secondary schools, 74 percent of students want to keep studying, but only 16 percent know what to study. Eligiendo Mi Camino gives every final-year student an AI career coach during the school day, in the school's computer lab: twelve sessions, with official wages for more than 130 occupations, ending in a personal plan.

110public schools in Lima, about 6,600 students, in a pre-registered randomized trial
9 in 10treated students used the coach
+0.12 SDin career readiness at midline
+14%in intentions to enroll in a technical institute, about 3 percentage points

Students' estimates of technical wages also moved about 21 percent closer to the official figure. These are plans and beliefs; enrollment comes later.

The math tutor: which design makes learning last

The same trial takes up the tutor debate above. The 110 schools were randomly assigned to three groups:

CControl: no tutor.
T1An AI math tutor that never gives the answer and guides the student step by step.
T2The same tutor plus three short moments where students regulate themselves: retrying earlier problems without hints, predicting their own errors, and checking worked solutions.

We expect T1 and T2 to beat C while students use them; the question is which gains remain once the tutor is removed, because that tells a ministry which design to buy. The endline is in the field.

If the results hold, there is demand to scale: four Peruvian regions have asked for the program in all 1,662 of their public secondary schools in 2027, about 348,000 students. Ecuador is building its own version, and an offline version runs in schools without internet.

04 · Whole systems

El Salvador, and how to check a platform before a trial exists

El Salvador is changing everything at once: connectivity and devices, AI learning platforms, support for teachers and principals, and measurement, aiming to go from 10 schools to 5,000 in three years. At the request of Bank management, we gave the government technical feedback on the AI platforms it is deploying, without picking a vendor. The ones we reviewed in depth shared the same gaps: wrong answers are not linked to the misconception behind them, nothing separates what a student solved alone from what the AI did, and there is no protected practice from memory.

evalkit

Most products a ministry buys will never have a trial, so we built evalkit, a team of AI agents that tests a platform the way a student would. The ministry says what the platform is for; the agents turn that into criteria and test cases, a person approves the plan, and the agents then drive a real browser through the real product, several times, because AI tutors answer differently each time. Every score cites the screenshot behind it, and anything sensitive, such as a student in distress, needs a human signature before it is tested.

In one run, a demo math tutor was given three wrong sums and answered all three with the same generic sentence, without ever naming the error. evalkit checks behavior, not learning: knowing whether these platforms raise learning in El Salvador needs a randomized trial, which we do not have the resources to run today.

05 · The evidence in one place

EvidencIA

Knowledge about AI and learning is split across researchers, universities, governments, companies and schools. EvidencIA is the Latin American network on AI and the science of learning that the Bank convenes to bring them together, with partners including Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, Tec de Monterrey and MetaRed, a network of 945 universities.

It keeps a living library of 1,847 documents, each labeled by what it can support; 94 are randomized trials or a meta-analysis that measure learning on a test taken without AI, each figure checked against the paper. A free connector puts that library inside Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini, so an official or a teacher who asks an AI assistant gets an answer built on studies, with the strength of the evidence and what is still unknown.

Resources

Done on small budgets, one grant at a time

Every evaluation on this page was done with small budgets, by applying for the specific grants that existed at the time: support from Anthropic and Microsoft, the Human Capital Project, and the World Bank Group President's Innovation Award. What we lack is funding for the next steps: a randomized trial of the platforms in El Salvador, the follow-up of the 2026 Lima cohort into further education and earnings, and the trial of El Director LIBRE.
