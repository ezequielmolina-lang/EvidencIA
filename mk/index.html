What we know so far

AI can help or harm learning. The design decides which.

In Turkey, high school students given free access to GPT-4 scored 17 percent lower on an exam taken without it; a version with guardrails removed the harm but produced no gain. When guidance is built in, results turn positive: university students in Chile told how to use AI as a tutor gained 0.22 SD on the final exam, and lower secondary students in Sierra Leone gained 0.26 SD in math with lessons on Gemini's guided mode.

On average, our background paper for the WDR 2026 finds that adaptive and AI-enabled tools raise learning by 0.125 SD across 14 randomized trials, and that generative AI cannot yet be told apart from the adaptive software that came before it.

01 · School leaders

El Director LIBRE

In the latest survey of school leaders, 75 percent of the 1,615 respondents in Latin America (Argentina, Brazil, Chile and Colombia) say they spend most of their time on administrative and management tasks. El Director LIBRE is a free World Bank book with a five-step method for using AI to cut that administrative time so principals can spend it on instructional leadership. Peru's Ministry of Education used it to train about 6,000 new principals.

We have now built it as software, with tools for the paperwork principals actually do (school council minutes, circulars, letters to families), and a version for El Salvador that records how principals use their time. We are now testing the platform with principals so that we can then evaluate it with a randomized trial.

02 · Teachers

La IA en el Aula: training teachers to use AI

We tested whether training teachers to use AI saves them time. In a randomized trial with 1,269 sixth-grade teachers in 390 public schools in Lima, one day of training, an AI license and weekly messages raised use of the license by 20 percentage points. It saved no detectable time on any of seven main tasks, but teachers wrote better prompts and rejected more weak AI answers, and older teachers gained the most. The lesson for governments: do not buy AI for teachers on the promise of time savings.

Teach, the Bank's classroom observation tool.

Teach + IA: feedback after every lesson

Teach is the World Bank's tool for observing a lesson and scoring how well the teacher teaches: nine practices, such as checking that students understand or giving them feedback, each scored from 1 to 5. Today it needs a trained, certified person sitting in the classroom, which is expensive, so most teachers are observed rarely, if ever. We asked whether AI could do the scoring from a recording of the lesson. Using 199 lessons recorded in Peru, we gave a language model the same exam human observers must pass to be certified. With the detailed guide used to train human observers, it passed 93 percent of simulated exams; with a short version of the rules, only 57 percent. The model was the same; what changed was the guide. The expertise is in the rubric. Next step: a tool that records a lesson and returns feedback to the teacher, tested with a randomized trial on teaching practice and student learning.

03 · Students

Eligiendo Mi Camino

At the start of the Lima trial, 74 percent of final-year students wanted to keep studying, but only 16 percent knew what to study. Eligiendo Mi Camino gives every final-year student an AI career coach during the school day, in the school's computer lab: twelve sessions, with official wages for more than 130 occupations, ending in a personal plan.

110public schools in Lima, about 6,600 students, pre-registered randomized trial
9 in 10treated students used the coach
+0.12 SDin career readiness at midline
+14%in intentions to enroll in a technical institute, about 3 points

The coach also raised students' estimates of what a technical career pays by about 21 percent, toward the official figure. These are plans and beliefs; enrollment comes later.

The math tutor: which design makes learning last

The same trial takes up the tutor debate. Among the randomized trials of generative AI tutors in the EvidencIA library, it is the largest and the longest in a developing country, and the largest anywhere by number of schools. The 110 schools were randomly assigned to three groups:

Both treatment groups use the same tutor, and it is diagnostic-first: instead of waiting for students to ask, it asks them, from a bank of about 3,950 questions on the grade 5 curriculum in which every wrong option is mapped to a specific misconception. When a student gets one wrong, the tutor knows what she does not understand and guides her with a question, never giving the answer, while a dashboard lets the teacher follow each student.

CControl, 22 schools: regular math classes, no tutor.
T1The standard tutor, 44 schools: the AI does the regulating for the student. It decides what she practices next, catches her errors and judges when she has mastered a topic.
T2The same tutor with added friction, 44 schools: from week 5, about four minutes per session hand that metacognitive work back to the student. She predicts her own errors before seeing the solution and judges whether a worked solution is right. It feels harder; the bet is that it lasts.

Both run during regular school hours, in half of the weekly math classes, as practice in the school's computer lab (the aula de innovación pedagógica). We trained the math teachers for two days, in a practical way, gave them ongoing support, and sent them reports on how each class and each student was doing.

We expect T1 and T2 to beat C while students use them; the question is which gains remain once the tutor is removed, because that tells a ministry which design to buy. The main test is a math exam taken without AI four months after the tutor is removed, in November 2026 (pre-registration: The Personalization Trap). If the results hold, there is demand to scale: four Peruvian regions have asked for the program in all 1,662 of their public secondary schools in 2027, about 348,000 students. Ecuador is building its own version, and an offline version runs in schools without internet.

04 · Whole systems

El Salvador, and how to check a platform before a trial exists

El Salvador is modernizing its whole education system at once: connectivity and devices that reach the classroom, AI learning platforms with quality content, training and coaching for teachers and principals, and assessment that comes back in time to act on it. It was designed as a gradual process, with learning as the reference point: a pilot in 10 schools, about 200 by mid-2025, nearly 1,000 today, and on the way to almost 5,000 public schools.

evalkit: a team of AI agents that tests a platform

Most products a ministry buys will never have a trial. In evalkit, the ministry says what the platform is for; AI agents turn that into criteria and test cases, a person approves the plan, and the agents then drive a real browser through the real product, several times, because AI tutors answer differently each time. Every score cites the screenshot behind it, and anything sensitive, such as a student in distress, needs a human signature before it is tested.

evalkit checks behavior, not learning: knowing whether El Salvador's program raises learning needs a randomized trial of the program, which we do not have the resources to run today.

Does not meet · probed 3 times

After a pupil's arithmetic error, the tutor names what the error is, not just that it is wrong. Three wrong sums were put to a demo math tutor: 27+45=62, 6×4=28, 52−27=35. All three times it replied with the same sentence: "tell me which part doesn't add up and we'll look at it together".

05 · The evidence in one place

EvidencIA

Knowledge about AI and learning is split across researchers, universities, governments, companies and schools. EvidencIA is the Latin American network on AI and the science of learning that the Bank convenes to bring them together, with partners including Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, Tec de Monterrey and MetaRed, a network of 945 universities.

A living library, sorted by what each document can support

1,848documents, 16 October 2026
94 causal on learning, each figure checked against the paper: 69 randomized trials of AI, 24 of teaching without AI (the science of learning) and one meta-analysis; learning always tested without AI 242 causal, with limits 933 descriptive 579 policy and guidance

All the AI experiments in one chart, and every number can be opened

The library pools every randomized experiment of generative AI on learning in one chart, from largest to smallest effect, on a common scale. One tap on a study shows how its effect was computed: the formula, the figures it uses, and the page and exact quote from the paper they come from. Anyone can check the evidence behind an answer instead of taking it on trust.

A free connector that puts the library inside Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini

Done on small budgets, one grant at a time.

Every evaluation on this page was done with small budgets, by applying for the specific grants that existed at the time: support from Anthropic and Microsoft, the Human Capital Project, and the World Bank Group President's Innovation Award. What we lack is stable funding to sustain and expand this research agenda, so that the next steps do not depend on which grants we happen to win: a randomized trial of the program in El Salvador, the follow-up of the 2026 Lima cohort into further education and earnings, and the trial of El Director LIBRE.
